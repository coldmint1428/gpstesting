Intro, roles and how it works

On screen
  • Home page, then the Events list
  • Over "Under the hood": one slide, website and app → Supabase database → OneMap
Say

Hi, today we're demonstrating Planova, an app for running big outdoor events, like an NDP rehearsal with dozens of contingents moving around one venue. You plan where every group needs to be and when, and on the day you see where they actually are.

What you see depends on your role. The root created the event and can do everything. Planners draw the map, build the timeline and manage people. Everyone else is in a group, led by a group admin and at least one IC, whose phone shares location. Members only see their own group.

Everything lives in a Supabase database, which also enforces those roles, and maps come from OneMap's public API. We're signed in as root.

Build the timeline

Say

Now the plan itself. A plan is made of frames, each one a moment counted from T+0, when the event clock starts. In the first frame, press Place on map beside each group and tap where they stand. Add a frame five minutes in and drag the badges to the formation area. The app works out the walk in between. The panel on the right shifts a frame or holds a group a few minutes longer.

A real rehearsal is hundreds of moments, so run sheets come in as a CSV. This one is the NE4 show matrix: thirteen groups, four hundred and eighteen moments. A copy with one impossible jump is refused, with the row number. Press Play, and every contingent walks its route on the plan's clock.

Two planners can edit at once. When my colleague drags a badge, it moves on my screen too, held for them until they let go. When it's ready we publish, and that's the version every phone follows.

What members and ICs see

On screen
  • The Contingent 3 IC's account: the side menu
  • Schedule: the map and stop times, a countdown, LATE; tap We're here
  • Map, then My group
  • IC's phone: Share my location, the set-up screens; lock the phone; pull down the notification
Say

Here's what an IC sees; members see the same pages, minus location sharing. The side menu has Overview, Live, Schedule, Rehearsals, Map, My group and Attendance.

The Schedule is a map and list of where their group should be, with stop times and a countdown to the next one. Once a moment passes and nobody's marked the group, it shows late. When they arrive, the IC taps We're here, which records how late they were. Map shows their route, and My group shows their people, IC and admin.

Once the event's live, the IC taps Share my location. The first time, the app walks them through the phone settings one screen at a time. Then it keeps sharing with the screen off, with a notification and its own Stop button. And when the IC's phone gets close enough, the group is marked arrived on its own.

Will it fit in 12 minutes?

Yes, but only with a tight edit. The narration is about 1,250 words, roughly 9:00 at 140 words a minute. The other 2:40 is for clicks, so the clicking has to happen while you talk, as the script's "type as you talk" asks. Every segment's lines fit inside its slot.

Keep it inside the slot

  • Record each segment as its own clip and time it. Cut, don't re-explain.
  • Cut every spinner and page load. Speed up typing and the test run 2 to 4 times.
  • Sign every account in before you record.
  • Live segments: keep the phone recording throughout, and use only the moments that matter.

If the cut runs long, trim in this order

  1. Groups and people (25 s): drop it; say "groups can be edited any time" in segment 2.
  2. Simulator (40 s → 20 s): the moving dots only; mention the planner phone in one line.
  3. Playback (60 s → 40 s): skip the trails and Follow.
  4. Members and ICs (80 s → 60 s): skip Map and My group.
  5. Rehearsals (20 s → 10 s): the calendar only.

Never cut the intro, the timeline, the live segments, SOS or testing. The intro and testing segments carry what the brief marks.

Recording your lines

  • Play your clip and talk over it with your segment's lines from the run sheet above. A phone voice memo is fine; record somewhere quiet.
  • One audio file per segment, named by number and speaker: 05 Ern.m4a. MP3, M4A and WAV all work.

  • Running longer than the clip is fine: the last frame holds until you finish. Running far over the slot isn't, as the whole video must stay under 12:00.

How it's put together
