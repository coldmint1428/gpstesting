Intro, roles and how it works

On screen
  • Home page, then the Events list
  • Over "Under the hood": one slide, website and app → Supabase database → OneMap
Say

Hi, this is Planova. Picture an NDP rehearsal: dozens of contingents moving around one venue at the same time. Planova lets you plan where every group should be and when, and then, on the day, see where they actually are. What you can do depends on your role. The root sets up the event and can do everything. Planners draw the map, build the timeline and manage people. Everyone else is in a group, led by an admin and at least one IC, whose phone shares its location. Members only see their own group. Behind it all is a Supabase database, which also enforces those roles, and the maps come from OneMap. We're signed in as root, so let's make an event.

Build the timeline

On screen
  • Timeline: new plan "Run of show"; Place on map for two groups, tap the map
  • Add frame, +5:00; drag both badges; the right panel's Shift time and Hold for
  • Import CSV: ndp-ne4-demo-broken.csv is refused at row 31; then ndp-ne4-demo.csv
  • Play at 60× for 10 s: contingents marching
  • Second browser (planner): drag a badge; on root's screen it moves too

Say

Now for the plan itself. A plan is a series of frames, and each frame is a moment counted from T+0, when the event clock starts. In the first frame, we press Place on map next to each group and tap where they're standing. Then we add a frame five minutes in and drag the badges over to the formation area. The app works out the walk in between, and the panel on the right lets us shift a frame or hold a group back a few minutes. A real rehearsal has hundreds of moments, so we import the run sheet as a CSV. This is the actual NE4 show matrix: thirteen groups, four hundred and eighteen moments. A file with an impossible jump, like this copy, is refused, and it tells you which row. Press Play, and every contingent walks its route on the plan's clock. Two planners can work on it together, too. When my teammate drags a badge, I see it move, held for them until they let go. Then we publish, and that's the version every phone follows.

What members and ICs see

On screen

Say

So what does an IC see? Members get the same pages, just without location sharing. In the menu there's Overview, Live, Schedule, Rehearsals, My group and Attendance. The Schedule shows where their group should be, with their route on a map and a list of stops, and a countdown to the next one. If a moment passes and nobody's marked the group, it shows as late. When they get there, the IC taps We're here, which also records how late they were. My group shows their people, IC and admin. Once the event's live, the IC taps Share my location. The first time, the app walks them through their phone settings, one screen at a time. After that, it keeps sharing even with the screen off, with a notification and its own Stop button. And when the IC gets close enough, the group's marked as arrived automatically.

Will it fit in 12 minutes?

Yes, but only with a tight edit. The narration is about 1,200 words, roughly 8:35 at 140 words a minute. The other 2:45 is for clicks, so the clicking has to happen while you talk, as the script's "type as you talk" asks. Every segment's lines fit inside its slot.

Keep it inside the slot

  • Record each segment as its own clip and time it. Cut, don't re-explain.
  • Cut every spinner and page load. Speed up typing and the test run 2 to 4 times.
  • Sign every account in before you record.
  • Live segments: keep the phone recording throughout, and use only the moments that matter.

If the cut runs long, trim in this order

  1. Groups and people (25 s): drop it; say "groups can be edited any time" in segment 2.
  2. Playback (60 s → 40 s): skip the trails and Follow.
  3. Members and ICs (80 s → 60 s): skip Map and My group.
  4. Rehearsals (20 s → 10 s): the calendar only.

Never cut the intro, the timeline, the live segments, SOS or testing. The intro and testing segments carry what the brief marks.

Recording your lines

  • Play your clip and talk over it with your segment's lines from the run sheet above. A phone voice memo is fine; record somewhere quiet.
  • One audio file per segment, named as in the table: the segment number, a space, then your name, such as 05 Ern.m4a. Keep the two-digit number (05, not 5). MP3, M4A and WAV all work; only the extension changes.
  • Komin records 01, 03, 08, 10, 12 and 15; Ern 02, 05, 06, 07 and 11; Linus 04; Hazel 09; rui 13 and 14.
  • Start talking within a second of pressing record; the start of your file lines up with the start of the clip.
  • Running longer than the clip is fine: the last frame holds until you finish. Running far over the slot isn't, as the whole video must stay under 12:00.
